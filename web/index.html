      Contango: later expirations trade above nearer ones; the norm in a calm market. Backwardation: nearer expirations trade richer; traders pay up for immediate protection, and it tends to coincide with selloffs.

      CFTC Traders in Financial Futures report, futures only. Positions as of Tuesday's close, published Friday at 15:30 ET (21:30 in Barcelona; 20:30 in the weeks when the European and US clock changes don't line up). Net = longs − shorts, in contracts. The index places the current net within its range (0 = the shortest of the period, 100 = the longest); above 90 or below 10 it is flagged as extreme. In bonds, leveraged funds are structurally short because of the basis trade (they buy the cash bond and sell the future) and asset managers are long: compare each group with its own range, not with its sign. Click a market to see its history.

      Real-time Tradier options: SPX, NDX, RUT, SPY, QQQ and IWM are recalculated every 5 minutes from 07:00 ET to the close, with 1-minute candles in TRACE, shown at 1, 5 or 15 minutes (before the open, the ETF's extended-hours candles, scaled to the index); everything else every 15 minutes, with CBOE candles (~15 min delay). If Tradier fails, CBOE is used. Open interest is from the previous close. Dealer sign follows the standard convention: long calls, short puts. The Volatility Trigger (Hedge Wall for stocks) is our own approximation, not the SpotGamma level.
